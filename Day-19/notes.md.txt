# SQL Day 19 - Formatting and Casting Date & Time Functions

## Why Format Dates?

Databases store dates in standard formats.

Example:

2026-05-30 22:45:10

Users usually want:

30/05/2026

or

May 30, 2026

Formatting functions help display dates in user-friendly formats.

---

# CAST()

Purpose:

Convert one datatype into another datatype.

Syntax:

CAST(expression AS datatype)

Examples:

CAST(GETDATE() AS DATE)

Output:

2026-05-30

---

CAST(GETDATE() AS TIME)

Output:

22:45:10

---

Use Cases:

- Remove time from datetime
- Extract date only
- Data conversion

---

# CONVERT()

Purpose:

Convert and format values.

Syntax:

CONVERT(datatype, expression, style)

Examples:

Style 101

MM/DD/YYYY

Style 103

DD/MM/YYYY

Style 23

YYYY-MM-DD

Example:

CONVERT(VARCHAR, GETDATE(), 103)

Output:

30/05/2026

---

# FORMAT()

Purpose:

Custom formatting of date and time.

Syntax:

FORMAT(date, format_string)

Examples:

FORMAT(GETDATE(), 'dd/MM/yyyy')

Output:

30/05/2026

---

FORMAT(GETDATE(), 'MMMM')

Output:

May

---

FORMAT(GETDATE(), 'dddd')

Output:

Friday

---

FORMAT(GETDATE(), 'dd MMMM yyyy')

Output:

30 May 2026

---

# Difference Between CAST, CONVERT and FORMAT

CAST()

- Data type conversion only
- ANSI SQL standard
- Faster

CONVERT()

- Conversion + predefined styles
- SQL Server specific

FORMAT()

- Fully customizable
- Most readable
- Slightly slower

---

# Common Formatting Patterns

yyyy
→ 2026

MM
→ 05

dd
→ 30

MMMM
→ May

dddd
→ Friday

HH:mm:ss
→ 22:45:10

dd/MM/yyyy
→ 30/05/2026

dd MMM yyyy
→ 30 May 2026
