# SQL Day 17 - Date Part Extraction Functions

## Introduction

Date Part Extraction functions are used to retrieve specific components from a date or datetime value.

Instead of working with the complete date, we can extract:

- Year
- Month
- Day
- Hour
- Minute
- Second

These functions are commonly used in reporting, analytics, dashboards, and filtering records.

---

## YEAR()

Returns the year portion from a date.

Syntax:

YEAR(date_column)

Example:

YEAR('2026-05-28')

Output:

2026

Use Cases:
- Annual reports
- Employee joining year
- Sales by year

---

## MONTH()

Returns the month portion from a date.

Syntax:

MONTH(date_column)

Example:

MONTH('2026-05-28')

Output:

5

Use Cases:
- Monthly sales reports
- Monthly registrations
- Monthly analytics

---

## DAY()

Returns the day portion from a date.

Syntax:

DAY(date_column)

Example:

DAY('2026-05-28')

Output:

28

Use Cases:
- Daily reporting
- Event scheduling
- Daily transactions

---

## DATEPART()

DATEPART() extracts a specific component from a date or datetime value.

Syntax:

DATEPART(part, date_column)

Examples:

DATEPART(YEAR, hire_date)

DATEPART(MONTH, hire_date)

DATEPART(DAY, hire_date)

DATEPART(HOUR, event_datetime)

DATEPART(MINUTE, event_datetime)

---

## Why DATEPART()?

YEAR(), MONTH(), and DAY() are specialized functions.

DATEPART() is more flexible because it can extract:

- Year
- Month
- Day
- Hour
- Minute
- Second
- Week
- Quarter

from the same date value.

---

## Practical Applications

- Employee joining year analysis
- Monthly order tracking
- Daily transaction reports
- Event time analysis
- Dashboard filtering
- Business reporting
