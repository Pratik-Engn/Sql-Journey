# Problems Day 2

## Question 13 — Count Users Registered per Contest

You are given a `Register` table containing contest registrations.

Columns:
- `contest_id`
- `user_id`

Find how many users participated in each contest.

Return:
- `contest_id`
- `total_users`

Calculate the percentage of registered users only if the question explicitly asks for it. For this problem, return the total number of distinct registered users in each contest.

Sort the result by `contest_id` in ascending order.

## Question 14 — Was First Order Delivered On Time

You are given a `Delivery` table containing customer orders and their preferred delivery dates.

Columns:
- `order_id`
- `customer_id`
- `order_date`
- `customer_pref_delivery_date`

For each customer, find their first order (the order with the earliest `order_date`).

Check whether that first order was delivered on the customer's preferred date.

Return:
- `customer_id`
- `delivered_on_time`

Return `Yes` when the first order date equals the preferred delivery date; otherwise, return `No`.

Sort by `customer_id` in ascending order.

---

## Question 15 — Category Employees by Salary

You are given an `Employee` table containing employee salary information.

Columns:
- `id`
- `name`
- `salary`

Categorize each employee according to their salary:

- `Low`: salary less than 4000
- `Medium`: salary between 4000 and 7000, inclusive
- `High`: salary greater than 7000

Return:
- `id`
- `category`

## Question 16 — Queries Quality and Percentage

### Queries
- `query_id`
- `user_id`
- `score`

### Users
- `user_id`
- `user_name`

For each user, calculate the percentage of their queries that have a score greater than or equal to 60.

Round the percentage to two decimal places.

Return:
- `user_id`
- `user_name`
- `quality_percentage`

## Question 17 — Students and Examinations

You are given three tables.

### Students
- `student_id`
- `student_name`

### Subjects
- `subject_id`
- `subject_name`

### Examinations
- `student_id`
- `subject_id`

For every student and every subject, count how many times the student attended that subject's examination.

Include every student-subject combination, even when the student never attended the examination.

Return:
- `student_id`
- `student_name`
- `subject_id`
- `subject_name`
- `attended_exams`

Sort by `student_id`, then `subject_id`.

## Question 24 — Count Salary Categories

You are given an `Employee` table containing:

- `id`
- `name`
- `salary`

Count the number of employees in each salary category:

- `Low`: salary less than 4000
- `Medium`: salary greater than or equal to 4000 and less than or equal to 7000
- `High`: salary greater than 7000

Return:
- `category`
- `employee_count`

Use `UNION ALL` to produce the three categories, including categories with zero employees.

Sort the result in this order: `High`, `Low`, `Medium`.
